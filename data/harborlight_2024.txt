COMPANY: Harborlight Logistics
YEAR: 2024

[PAGE 5]
Harborlight Logistics is a European freight forwarding and contract logistics group. The
company arranges ocean, air and road freight on behalf of industrial and retail customers
and operates 22 warehouses with a combined 610,000 square metres of storage capacity. The
group does not own vessels or aircraft and purchases capacity from carriers, which means
reported revenue moves with prevailing freight rates. Approximately 58 percent of gross
revenue relates to purchased transport capacity. The group employed 6,400 people at year end
and served 3,100 active customers, the largest of which represented 4 percent of net
revenue.

[PAGE 9]
Gross revenue increased to EUR 2.85 billion in 2024 from EUR 2.61 billion in 2023, while net
revenue, defined as gross revenue less purchased transport capacity, increased to EUR 1.20
billion from EUR 1.14 billion. The divergence reflects the pass-through of higher container
rates to customers. Ocean freight volumes measured in twenty-foot equivalent units grew 3
percent, air freight tonnage grew 9 percent, and contract logistics revenue grew 7 percent
supported by two new warehouse contracts in the Netherlands.

[PAGE 12]
Purchased transport capacity cost EUR 1.65 billion, an increase of 12 percent. Container
spot rates on Asia to North Europe routes rose sharply during the second quarter following
capacity disruption on Red Sea routings, and average rates for the full year were 34 percent
above the prior year. The group was able to pass through the majority of these higher
freight costs to customers under index-linked contracts, although a lag of approximately six
weeks applies to most agreements. Air freight rates rose 11 percent as shippers switched
from ocean to air for time-critical cargo, a shift the group also observed among its
industrial manufacturing customers.

[PAGE 15]
Adjusted EBITDA increased to EUR 214 million from EUR 196 million, and adjusted EBITDA
margin on net revenue was 17.8 percent compared with 17.2 percent. Margin improvement was
driven by higher air freight gross profit per shipment and by warehouse automation
investments completed in 2023. Contract logistics margins were stable. The group notes that
its margin on net revenue is a more meaningful measure than margin on gross revenue, since
the latter is distorted by movements in purchased transport rates.

[PAGE 18]
Energy and fuel costs increased by EUR 23 million year on year. Diesel represented the
largest component, reflecting the group's owned road fleet of 480 vehicles, while warehouse
electricity costs rose following the expiry of fixed-price contracts in three countries.
Higher energy prices were partially offset by a fuel surcharge mechanism applied to road
freight customers, which recovered approximately 70 percent of the increase. The group
continued its programme of converting warehouse lighting and refrigeration to lower
consumption equipment, and reduced electricity use per square metre by 6 percent.

[PAGE 22]
Management expects container rates to normalise during 2025 as new vessel capacity enters
service, which would reduce both gross revenue and purchased transport cost with limited net
revenue impact. Guidance for 2025 is net revenue growth of 3 to 6 percent and an adjusted
EBITDA margin on net revenue of 17 to 19 percent. Management expects continued volume growth
in contract logistics and has committed capital expenditure of EUR 55 million to two
automated warehouse projects.

[PAGE 25]
Principal risks are freight rate volatility, carrier capacity availability, fuel and energy
price exposure, customer credit risk in the retail segment, and regulatory change relating
to emissions reporting for transport services. The group is exposed to counterparty risk
where it commits to carrier capacity in advance of customer commitments. Emissions reporting
obligations under European regulation expanded during the year and the group invested in
carrier-level emissions data collection to meet customer reporting requirements.
