COMPANY: BluePeak Software
YEAR: 2024

[PAGE 3]
BluePeak Software provides cloud-based treasury and cash management software to mid-market
and enterprise customers, primarily in Northern Europe and the DACH region. The platform is
delivered as a multi-tenant subscription service and is sold in three editions, Core,
Advanced and Enterprise, with optional modules for payment factory, in-house banking and
FX exposure management. At year end the group served 1,340 customers, of which 214 were
classified as enterprise accounts contributing more than EUR 250,000 of annual recurring
revenue each. The group employed 1,120 people across offices in Stockholm, Amsterdam,
Frankfurt and Kraków. Approximately 84 percent of revenue was subscription revenue, with
the remainder from implementation services and training.

[PAGE 8]
Revenue increased to EUR 620 million in 2024, up from EUR 540 million in 2023, representing
growth of 14.8 percent. Growth was driven by enterprise subscriptions and expansion in
Northern Europe, where the group added 46 new enterprise customers during the year.
Subscription revenue grew 17.2 percent while services revenue was broadly flat, reflecting
a deliberate shift of implementation work to certified partners. Revenue from the DACH
region grew 21 percent and now represents 27 percent of group revenue. Deferred revenue at
year end was EUR 268 million, up 16 percent, providing visibility into the following year.

[PAGE 11]
Annual recurring revenue reached EUR 548 million at 31 December 2024, up from EUR 462
million. Net revenue retention was 114 percent compared with 111 percent in the prior year,
reflecting both seat expansion within existing accounts and module cross-sell. Gross
revenue retention was 96 percent. Average annual recurring revenue per customer increased
to EUR 409,000 from EUR 361,000. The group reports a customer acquisition cost payback
period of 19 months, an improvement from 24 months, driven by a higher share of inbound and
partner-sourced pipeline. Churn was concentrated in the Core edition among customers with
fewer than 200 employees.

[PAGE 14]
Adjusted EBITDA increased to EUR 136 million from EUR 108 million. Adjusted EBITDA margin
improved from 20.0 percent to 21.9 percent due to higher recurring revenue and lower
customer acquisition costs. Sales and marketing expense fell to 28 percent of revenue from
31 percent, as partner-sourced deals carried lower direct selling cost and paid acquisition
spend was reduced. Gross margin improved by 90 basis points to 79.4 percent, helped by
migration of the remaining single-tenant customers onto the multi-tenant platform.
Partially offsetting these gains, cloud infrastructure expense grew faster than revenue,
rising 19 percent year on year, and research and development expense increased to EUR 118
million from EUR 99 million. Items excluded from adjusted EBITDA are share-based
compensation of EUR 21 million and a EUR 4 million charge related to office consolidation
in Amsterdam.

[PAGE 17]
Research and development expense was EUR 118 million, equivalent to 19.0 percent of
revenue, compared with 18.3 percent in the prior year. Spend was directed primarily at the
payment factory module, a rebuilt reconciliation engine, and machine learning features for
cash forecasting. Capitalised development costs were EUR 14 million, amortised over three
years. Cloud infrastructure expense was EUR 62 million. The group completed migration of
its European workloads to a single hyperscale provider during the third quarter, and
management expects the resulting committed-use discounts to moderate infrastructure cost
growth from 2025.

[PAGE 19]
Management expects continued growth in enterprise subscriptions in 2025, while noting that
cloud infrastructure costs remain an important margin sensitivity. Guidance for 2025 is
revenue growth of 12 to 15 percent and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 22 to 24 percent.
Management intends to hold sales and marketing expense broadly flat as a share of revenue
and to fund further research and development from gross margin improvement. The guidance
assumes no material change in enterprise software buying patterns and excludes the effect
of any acquisitions.

[PAGE 23]
Cash flow from operating activities was EUR 152 million compared with EUR 114 million in
2023. Free cash flow was EUR 131 million after capital expenditure and capitalised
development costs of EUR 21 million. The group held net cash of EUR 96 million at year end
and has no external borrowings other than lease liabilities of EUR 38 million. No dividend
is proposed; the board intends to retain earnings to fund product development and selective
acquisitions. The group repurchased EUR 25 million of shares during the year to offset
dilution from employee equity plans.

[PAGE 27]
Principal risks are customer concentration in the enterprise segment, dependence on a
single cloud infrastructure provider following the 2024 migration, information security and
data protection obligations arising from the processing of customer payment data,
competition from banking incumbents bundling treasury functionality, and the ability to
recruit and retain engineering staff. The group holds ISO 27001 certification and completed
a SOC 2 Type II examination during the year. A data protection impact assessment was
carried out ahead of the launch of the cash forecasting feature.

[PAGE 30]
Headcount grew to 1,120 from 980, with 62 percent of the increase in research and
development. Voluntary attrition was 11.4 percent, down from 14.1 percent. The Kraków
engineering centre grew to 310 people and now hosts the majority of platform
infrastructure work. Employee engagement scored 78 on the group's annual survey. Share-based
compensation of EUR 21 million covered 640 participating employees under a three-year
vesting programme.
