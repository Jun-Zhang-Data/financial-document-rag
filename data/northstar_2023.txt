COMPANY: Northstar Industrial
YEAR: 2023

[PAGE 4]
Northstar Industrial manufactures industrial pumps, valves and flow control systems for
European infrastructure and process industry customers. The group reports through two
segments, Industrial Systems and Aftermarket Services. During 2023 the group operated three
manufacturing plants, in Sweden, Germany and Spain, and began construction of a fourth
assembly facility in Poland intended to add capacity for standardised pump platforms from
2024. The group employed 3,940 people at year end. In November 2023 the group completed the
acquisition of Valveco, a Dutch supplier of control valves, for an enterprise value of EUR
78 million. The acquisition contributed one month of revenue to the reported period and is
integrated within the Industrial Systems segment.

[PAGE 10]
Revenue was EUR 1.39 billion in 2023, an increase of 7.9 percent on the prior year. Demand
remained strong across the company's main European markets, particularly in water and
wastewater infrastructure, where public investment programmes supported project activity.
Industrial Systems revenue was EUR 981 million and Aftermarket Services revenue was EUR 410
million. Price increases carried over from 2022 contributed approximately 3 percentage
points of growth, with volume contributing the remainder. Order backlog at year end was EUR
578 million. The group noted that lead times had shortened compared with the prior year as
component availability improved.

[PAGE 12]
Geographic revenue distribution was broadly unchanged. Germany represented 22 percent of
group revenue, the Nordic countries 24 percent, the Benelux region 12 percent, and the
United Kingdom and Switzerland together 17 percent. Export markets outside Europe accounted
for 11 percent, with the balance spread across southern and central Europe. The group
records revenue by customer delivery location rather than by contracting entity. No single
customer represented more than 6 percent of group revenue in either 2023 or 2022.

[PAGE 16]
Adjusted EBITDA was EUR 278 million and adjusted EBITDA margin was 20.0 percent, compared
with 18.6 percent in 2022. Profitability benefited from stable energy prices under a
favourable electricity hedge, improved factory utilisation, and the full-year effect of
price increases implemented during 2022. Freight rates declined during the year from the
elevated levels seen in 2022, contributing approximately EUR 9 million of cost relief.
Industrial Systems segment EBITDA was EUR 176 million and Aftermarket Services segment
EBITDA was EUR 108 million. The electricity hedge covering the majority of European
consumption was disclosed as expiring at the end of 2023, and management noted that
renewal would take place at higher prevailing market rates.

[PAGE 20]
For 2024 the group guided to organic revenue growth of 3 to 6 percent. Management noted
that the adjusted EBITDA margin was expected to be lower than the 2023 level because of the
electricity hedge expiry and start-up costs associated with the new Poland facility.
Management indicated that the Poland ramp-up would depress margins during the first three
quarters before contributing positively. The guidance assumed stable European industrial
demand and no renewed escalation in container freight rates.

[PAGE 24]
Management highlighted supply-chain reliability and cost control as key priorities for
2024. Specific initiatives included dual-sourcing of cast components, a reduction in the
number of active suppliers from 640 to approximately 500, and the introduction of a
consolidated inbound logistics contract intended to reduce freight spend. The group also
set a target to reduce energy consumption per unit produced by 8 percent over three years.
Progress against these initiatives is monitored monthly by the executive team.

[PAGE 28]
The board identified energy price exposure as the most significant near-term financial
risk, given the scheduled expiry of the electricity hedge. Other principal risks were
supply chain concentration, cyclical demand in industrial end-markets, integration risk
associated with the Valveco acquisition, and cyber security. The group's insurance
programme was renewed during the year with unchanged coverage limits.

[PAGE 31]
Cash flow from operating activities was EUR 231 million. Capital expenditure was EUR 68
million, including EUR 29 million of initial spend on the Poland facility. Free cash flow
was EUR 163 million. Net debt was EUR 389 million at year end following the Valveco
acquisition, equivalent to 1.4 times adjusted EBITDA. The board proposed a dividend of EUR
0.85 per share, an increase from EUR 0.78 in the prior year.
