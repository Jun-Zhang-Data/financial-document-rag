COMPANY: Northstar Industrial
YEAR: 2024

[PAGE 4]
Northstar Industrial is a European manufacturer of industrial pumps, valves and flow
control systems. The group operates through two reporting segments. Industrial Systems
designs and assembles pump and valve platforms for water utilities, chemical processing
and district energy customers. Aftermarket Services provides spare parts, retrofits,
condition monitoring and field maintenance for the installed base, which the group
estimates at approximately 240,000 active units. Manufacturing is concentrated in four
plants located in Sweden, Poland, Germany and Spain, supported by a new assembly facility
in Poland that began production during the second quarter of 2024. The group employed
4,180 people at year end and sells into 38 countries, with Germany, the Nordics and the
Benelux region representing the largest share of revenue. Approximately 71 percent of
revenue was generated in the European Union, 18 percent in the United Kingdom and
Switzerland, and the remainder across export markets in the Middle East and South East
Asia.

[PAGE 12]
Revenue increased to EUR 1.45 billion in 2024 from EUR 1.39 billion in 2023, corresponding
to growth of 4.3 percent. Organic growth was 3.1 percent, with the remainder attributable
to the full-year effect of the Valveco acquisition completed in late 2023 and a small
positive currency translation effect. Growth was driven primarily by higher sales volumes
in the Industrial Systems division, where order intake for water utility projects remained
strong throughout the year. Aftermarket Services grew 6.8 percent, benefiting from higher
service contract attachment rates and price increases implemented at the start of the
year. Order backlog at 31 December 2024 was EUR 612 million, up from EUR 578 million a
year earlier, and management considers approximately 80 percent of the backlog to be
deliverable within twelve months.

[PAGE 14]
Segment reporting is presented below. Industrial Systems generated revenue of EUR 1,012
million, up from EUR 981 million, and segment EBITDA of EUR 148 million, down from EUR 176
million. The decline reflects the cost of the plant ramp-up in Poland and higher inbound
freight on cast components sourced from third-party foundries. Aftermarket Services
generated revenue of EUR 438 million, up from EUR 410 million, and segment EBITDA of EUR
114 million, up from EUR 108 million, with margins broadly stable at 26.0 percent.
Unallocated group costs of EUR 16 million relate to central functions, insurance and
group-level IT programmes. Intersegment revenue is eliminated on consolidation and was
immaterial in both periods.

[PAGE 18]
Adjusted EBITDA decreased to EUR 246 million from EUR 278 million. Adjusted EBITDA margin
declined from 20.0 percent to 17.0 percent. Management attributed the decline mainly to
higher freight costs, higher energy prices, and temporary production inefficiencies during
the ramp-up of the new plant in Poland. Freight and logistics expense increased by EUR 19
million year on year, driven by container rate increases on Asian routes and a shift to
air freight for a limited number of critical components during the second quarter. Energy
costs increased by EUR 11 million despite lower consumption per unit produced, reflecting
the expiry of a favourable electricity hedge at the end of 2023. Ramp-up inefficiencies,
including scrap, rework and overtime at the Poland facility, are estimated at EUR 8
million. Price increases recovered approximately EUR 14 million of the total cost
headwind. Items excluded from adjusted EBITDA comprise restructuring charges of EUR 6
million and acquisition-related costs of EUR 2 million.

[PAGE 21]
The company expects logistics costs to normalise during 2025 as contracted container rates
reset and the temporary use of air freight ends. Management also expects efficiency
measures at the new plant to improve margins gradually over the course of the year, with
the majority of the benefit weighted to the second half. A new three-year electricity
contract covering approximately 60 percent of expected consumption was signed in November
2024. For 2025 the group guides to organic revenue growth of 2 to 5 percent and an
adjusted EBITDA margin of 18 to 19 percent. The guidance assumes no material deterioration
in European industrial demand and no further escalation in freight rates.

[PAGE 26]
Cash flow from operating activities was EUR 198 million compared with EUR 231 million in
2023. The reduction reflects lower earnings and a EUR 24 million increase in working
capital, principally inventory built ahead of the plant transition. Capital expenditure was
EUR 92 million, of which EUR 41 million related to the Poland facility. Free cash flow was
EUR 106 million, giving a cash conversion ratio of 43 percent of adjusted EBITDA. Net debt
was EUR 421 million at year end, equivalent to 1.7 times adjusted EBITDA, compared with 1.4
times a year earlier. The group's revolving credit facility of EUR 250 million was undrawn
at the balance sheet date and matures in 2028. The board proposes a dividend of EUR 0.85
per share, unchanged from the prior year.

[PAGE 29]
The principal risks identified by the board are input cost volatility, energy price
exposure, supply chain concentration, and cyclical demand in European industrial
end-markets. The group sources approximately 34 percent of cast components from three
foundries, and disruption at any of these suppliers could delay deliveries. Cyber security
risk has increased with the extension of remote condition monitoring to a larger share of
the installed base. Climate transition risk affects the group in two directions: carbon
pricing raises operating costs, while demand for energy-efficient pumping systems supports
the Aftermarket retrofit business. The audit committee reviewed the group's risk register
twice during the year.

[PAGE 33]
Revenue from the sale of goods is recognised when control transfers to the customer, which
is generally on delivery. Service contract revenue is recognised over the contract term as
the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefit. Adjusted EBITDA is a
non-IFRS measure defined as operating profit before depreciation, amortisation,
restructuring charges and acquisition-related costs. Management presents adjusted EBITDA
because it believes the measure improves comparability between periods, and a
reconciliation to operating profit is provided in note 6. Inventory is measured at the
lower of cost and net realisable value, with cost determined on a weighted average basis.

[PAGE 36]
The group employed 4,180 people at 31 December 2024 compared with 3,940 a year earlier,
with the increase concentrated in the Poland assembly facility and in Aftermarket field
service roles. Voluntary employee turnover was 9.2 percent. The lost time injury frequency
rate was 2.1 per million hours worked, an improvement from 2.6 in 2023. Women represented
29 percent of the total workforce and 24 percent of management positions. The group
completed a restructuring of its Spanish operations during the year, affecting 74
positions, and recognised related charges of EUR 6 million.
